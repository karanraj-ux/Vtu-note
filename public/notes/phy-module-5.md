## 1. Introduction to Nanomaterials
Nanomaterials are defined as materials having at least one dimension between $1\text{ nm}$ and $100\text{ nm}$ ($1\text{ nm} = 10^{-9}\text{ m}$).

### Dimensional Classification:
1. **0-D (Quantum Dots)**: All 3 dimensions in nanoscale ($x, y, z < 100\text{ nm}$). Discrete atomic-like energy spectra (CdSe, InAs quantum dots).
2. **1-D (Nanowires, Nanotubes)**: 2 dimensions in nanoscale; electrons free to move along 1 dimension (Carbon Nanotubes, ZnO nanowires).
3. **2-D (Nanosheets, Thin Films)**: 1 dimension in nanoscale; electrons free in 2 dimensions (Graphene, $\text{MoS}_2$ monolayers).
4. **3-D (Bulk Nanostructured Materials)**: Nanograined polycrystalline materials, nanocomposites.

## 2. Distinct Physical & Chemical Properties at Nanoscale
1. **Enormous Surface-to-Volume Ratio**:
   For a spherical nanoparticle of radius $r$:
   $$\frac{\text{Surface Area}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{4\pi r^2}{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3} = \frac{3}{r}$$
   As $r \to 0$, ratio explodes, leading to dramatically enhanced chemical reactivity and catalytic efficiency.
2. **Quantum Confinement Effect**:
   When crystal size becomes comparable to the exciton Bohr radius, continuous energy bands break up into discrete quantized energy levels:
   $$E_{\text{gap}}(R) = E_{\text{gap}}^{\text{bulk}} + \frac{\hbar^2 \pi^2}{2\mu R^2}$$
   Optical absorption and emission blue-shift with decreasing particle size.
3. **Lowered Melting Point**: Gibbs-Thomson effect causes nanoscale particles to melt at hundreds of degrees lower than bulk.

## 3. Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs) & Graphene
- **Graphene**: Single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a 2-D honeycomb $sp^2$-hybridized lattice.
  - Zero-gap semiconductor with linear Dirac dispersion ($E = \hbar v_F k$, $v_F \approx 10^6\text{ m/s}$).
  - Thermal conductivity $> 5000\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$, electron mobility up to $200,000\text{ cm}^2/\text{V}\cdot\text{s}$.
- **Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs)**: Graphene sheet rolled into cylindrical tube.
  - **Single-Walled (SWCNT)**: Single graphene cylinder ($d \approx 1-2\text{ nm}$).
  - **Multi-Walled (MWCNT)**: Multiple concentric cylinders separated by $0.34\text{ nm}$.
  - Chiral vector $C_h = n a_1 + m a_2$: If $(n - m)$ is divisible by 3, CNT is metallic; otherwise semiconducting.

## 4. Synthesis of Nanomaterials
- **Top-Down Approaches**: Ball milling, photolithography, chemical etching (breaking bulk down to nano).
- **Bottom-Up Approaches**:
  - **Sol-Gel Process**: Hydrolysis and condensation of metal alkoxides forming gel network followed by calcination.
  - **Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)**: Thermal decomposition of gaseous hydrocarbon precursors ($\text{CH}_4, \text{C}_2\text{H}_2$) over catalyst nanoparticles ($\text{Fe}, \text{Ni}, \text{Co}$) inside tube furnace.

## 5. Shock Waves: Physics & Reddy Shock Tube
### Definition of Shock Wave:
An extremely thin ($< 1\ \mu\text{m}$), large-amplitude pressure discontinuity propagating through a medium at supersonic speed ($M > 1$):
- **Mach Number ($M$)**: $M = \frac{u}{a}$, where $u$ is fluid velocity and $a$ is speed of sound.
- Across a shock front: Pressure, density, and temperature increase abruptly; velocity decreases.

### The Reddy Shock Tube (Hand-Operated Tabletop Shock Tube):
Invented by Prof. K.P.J. Reddy at Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore.
- **Structure**: Hand-driven bicycle pump or piston connected to a high-pressure driver section, separated from a low-pressure driven section by a thin plastic/aluminum diaphragm.
- **Operation**: Manual pumping builds pressure in driver section until diaphragm bursts. Bursting creates a planar supersonic shock wave ($M \approx 1.5-3$) propagating down driven tube.
- **Applications**: Cell transformation in biotechnology, needleless vaccine drug delivery, artificial diamond synthesis, kidney stone pulverization.
